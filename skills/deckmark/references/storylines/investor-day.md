## Investor Day / Capital Markets Day Deck

A listed company's multi-speaker deck that resets the equity story: strategy, segment deep dives, medium-term financial targets and capital allocation. It is a public disclosure; every number is on the record.

**Reader and decision.** Sell-side analysts who will rebuild their models that night, and buy-side portfolio managers deciding whether to own the stock. It drives **own**: re-rate the shares and believe the targets.

**Length.** 60 to 150 slides across speakers; each speaker block 10 to 25. A half-day event is the norm.

**Short form (a single-segment or results-day deck, 10 to 15 slides).** When the material covers one segment or one period: cover, disclaimer, the equity story as a statement, the segment or results in kpi-strip plus one chart each, the medium-term targets the material states (with the basis), capital allocation only if stated, close on the targets. Sections for segments or speakers the material does not cover are left out and listed on open items.

**Order.**
1. **cover** and **disclaimer**: safe harbour / forward-looking statements, non-GAAP measures note, market data sources.
2. **Agenda**: agenda-tracker with speaker roles and times.
3. **CEO: the equity story, 8-15 slides**: who we are kpi-strip; what we said last time vs what we delivered (two-column-compare, misses included); market and right to win (marimekko or ranked-bar); 3 to 5 strategic priorities; the medium-term targets preview.
4. **Segment deep dives, 10-20 slides each**: segment section-divider, segment kpi-strip, market growth and share, growth drivers sized, margin path waterfall-bridge, segment targets.
5. **Operations / technology / product**: proof points with numbers, customer evidence (quote-callout only if given and cleared).
6. **CFO: financial framework, 10-20 slides**: track record of growth, margin and returns; medium-term targets table (revenue growth, margin, EPS or FCF, ROIC, leverage, horizon year); bridge from current margin to target margin; capital allocation priorities (stacked-bar of cumulative uses: capex, M&A, dividends, buybacks) with the leverage policy; guidance for the current year.
7. **Summary**: the investment case in 3 to 5 claims.
8. **Q&A divider**.
9. **Appendix**: non-GAAP reconciliations, segment definitions, glossary.

**Lean form (default).** The model is the large-manager investor day: one idea a slide. Who we are as a col-row of proof numbers; growth as a big-compare or a ring-pair; the operating model as a `cycle`; an acquisition and what it enabled as a bracket; each section opened by a full-bleed statement; a takeaway-bar under every small table. Targets tables and reconciliations stay numeric.

**What practitioners expect that a model forgets.**
- Medium-term targets are a table with the metric, the definition, the base year, the target and the horizon. Analysts copy it straight into models.
- Each target has a bridge from today: levers sized so they sum to the gap.
- What was promised at the last investor day is scored honestly; missed targets are explained.
- Every non-GAAP figure has a reconciliation in the appendix.
- Capital allocation states the order of priority and the leverage range the company will hold.
- Each speaker's section opens with the same layout so the deck reads as one company.
- The bear case is addressed directly.

**Honesty rules.** Targets and guidance come only from the user. A forward-looking slide carries the safe-harbour marker. Market size and share figures need a named source and year. No customer logos, quotes or partner names unless given. The disclaimer is not optional.

**How it ends.** The investment case summary, then Q&A, then the appendix of reconciliations.

**Density.** Medium to sparse on stage (projected at large size, 20px body minimum); the PDF version for the website keeps the same slides. Tables of targets are the exception: dense and exact.

**Words.** 16:9 target 30-60, cap 80 · A4 cap 300 · no prose tables. Body words per content slide (decklint `wordy`, `prose-table`). Projected on stage: one big number or one chart and a line; show it, do not say it: one exhibit carries the slide, the takeaway is one line, detail goes to the appendix or the speaker notes.
