## Due Diligence Report (Financial / Commercial)

The adviser's findings for a buyer or lender on a target. What earnings really are, what working capital and debt really are, what the market says, and what each finding does to price or terms.

**Reader and decision.** The buyer's deal team, IC and lenders; the SPA lawyers read the debt-like items and the peg. It drives **price and protect**: confirm or adjust the bid, set the working capital peg, decide indemnities, escrow and conditions.

**Length.** Executive summary 5 to 10 pages; full report 40 to 120 pages, report or landscape format. Red-flag report 10 to 20.

**Short form (a red-flag or scope-limited report, 8 to 12 pages).** When diligence covered only part of the scope: cover, scope and basis (what was reviewed, what was not), key findings with one tag each, quality of earnings bridge, net debt and debt-like items, working capital, then only the workstreams completed, open items listing each workstream not covered and the documents it needs. Never a section of headings with nothing reviewed under them.

**Order.**
1. **cover**: project codename, report type (financial / commercial / red flag), draft or final, date, addressee, reliance status.
2. **disclaimer and scope**: what was done, what was not, information sources, reliance and limits.
3. **Key findings**: exec-summary-scr as a numbered list of findings, each with its value effect (EBITDA, net debt, NWC) and its tag: CHANGES THE DECISION / WORTH A QUESTION / EXPLAINED BY CONTEXT.
4. **Effect on value**: waterfall-bridge from the seller's headline to the diligence view (EBITDA adjustments times multiple, debt-like items, peg difference).
5. **Quality of earnings**: waterfall-bridge reported to management-adjusted to diligence-adjusted EBITDA; table of each adjustment with amount, basis, and accept / reject / partial.
6. **Revenue analysis**: customer concentration ranked-bar, churn and cohort, price vs volume split, revenue recognition notes.
7. **Cost and margin**: gross margin by product, overhead run-rate, headcount bridge.
8. **Net working capital**: monthly NWC over 24 months (line with average and seasonality), the proposed peg and its basis.
9. **Net debt and debt-like items**: table: financial debt, cash (trapped cash noted), and each debt-like item (deferred consideration, unpaid bonuses, tax, customer deposits, pensions, leases) with amount and whether it is agreed.
10. **Cash conversion and capex**: EBITDA to operating cash, maintenance vs growth capex.
11. **Business plan review**: projections vs history, assumption tests, a diligence sensitivity.
12. **Commercial section (if CDD)**: market size, growth, share, customer interviews (n, roles, dates), competitive matrix-2x2.
13. **Other matters**: tax, IT, systems, controls, as given.
14. **Appendix**: full statements, reconciliations, interview list by role, information request log.

**What practitioners expect that a model forgets.**
- Three pillars every time: QoE, NWC peg, net debt. Each finding ties to one of them and to price.
- Every adjustment has a basis and a source document reference; "management adjustment, not supported" is a valid status.
- Reported, management-adjusted and diligence-adjusted EBITDA are three separate columns.
- "Looks wrong, is normal": seasonal NWC swings, one-off costs that genuinely do not recur, new contracts with ramp-up margins. Test each flag against the normal list before raising it.
- Information not provided is listed in the request log, not treated as a finding against the target.
- Draft reports carry "Draft, subject to change" on every page.

**Honesty rules.** Neutral stance; no "the seller is hiding". A missing document is a question. Only numbers that do not tie and true integrity failures are hard blocks; a block stops the conclusion on that pillar while every other check still runs and is reported. Only a document closes an integrity gap, not a signed opinion. Interview quotes are verbatim and attributed by role.

**How it ends.** Summary of value effects and recommended SPA protections (peg, debt-like items list, specific indemnities, escrow), open items with owners, then the appendices.

**Density.** Dense. memo-page with inset tables; deck version one exhibit per slide with a findings sidebar.

**Words.** 16:9 target 100-130, cap 150 · A4 cap 350. Body words per content slide (decklint `wordy`). A judgment memo: findings with their effect on price or terms; show it, do not say it: one exhibit carries the slide, the takeaway is one line, detail goes to the appendix or the speaker notes.
