## Public-Sector Review (Value for Money / Programme Review)

An independent or internal review of how public money is being spent: whether a programme is economic, efficient and effective, and what should change. Read by people who will be held accountable for it in public.

**Reader and decision.** Ministers, permanent secretaries, finance and audit committees, oversight bodies and, often, the public and press. It drives **reform**: accept recommendations, re-scope or stop a programme, change funding, and commit to a response with dates.

**Length.** Summary 4 to 12 pages; full report 30 to 80 pages, portrait report. Briefing deck 10 to 20 slides.

**Short form (a briefing or interim report, 6 to 10 pages).** When the review is not complete: key facts page, what the review covered, findings to date numbered and each tied to its evidence, the figures the material carries (budget, spend to date, forecast cost) with their price base, what remains to be examined, no recommendations until the evidence supports them.

**Order.**
1. **cover**: title as a plain description of what was reviewed, the body that commissioned it, date, status (draft for factual accuracy check / final).
2. **Key facts page**: kpi-strip of the programme's essential numbers: budget, spend to date, timescale, people served, forecast cost at completion, each with source and date.
3. **Summary**: background in a paragraph; scope (what the review covered and did not); key findings numbered; conclusion on value for money in one paragraph; recommendations numbered and addressed to a named body.
4. **Part 1: background and context**: what the programme is for, its objectives as originally set, governance as an org-chart, funding flows.
5. **Part 2: cost and progress**: budget vs forecast over time (line), waterfall-bridge of cost growth from original estimate to current, milestones planned vs achieved timeline-gantt.
6. **Part 3: outcomes and effectiveness**: targets vs actual (two-column-compare or ranked-bar), benefits realised vs claimed, regional variation (map or small-multiples).
7. **Part 4: management and governance**: controls, data quality, risk management, rag-tracker against good-practice criteria.
8. **Conclusion on value for money**.
9. **Recommendations**: numbered table: recommendation, addressed to, timing, linked finding.
10. **Appendix**: methodology, evidence base (documents, interviews by role, data sets), glossary.

**What practitioners expect that a model forgets.**
- Paragraphs are numbered (1.1, 1.2) so a committee can cite them.
- Every figure states its source and whether it is outturn, forecast or estimate, and in which price base (nominal or real, which year).
- Findings are separated from conclusions; conclusions from recommendations. Each recommendation traces to a finding.
- Scope limits are stated up front, including what the reviewers could not access.
- Plain English: no consultancy jargon, acronyms spelt out at first use, written for a citizen reader.
- The audited body's factual accuracy check is part of the process; drafts say so.
- Balanced: what is working is reported with the same evidence standard as what is not.

**Honesty rules.** No figure without a source and price base. Benefit claims by the programme are reported as claims unless verified. Data gaps are findings about data quality, stated plainly, never filled. Individuals are not named; roles are.

**How it ends.** The recommendations table, then the methodology appendix. No marketing tone, no call to action beyond the recommendations.

**Density.** Medium. Report format, text-led, 11pt body, exhibits numbered "Figure 1" with source beneath. Slide version: sparse, one finding per slide.

**Words.** 16:9 target 80-110, cap 130 · A4 cap 350. Body words per content slide (decklint `wordy`). Report format, text-led; findings and recommendations carry the words; show it, do not say it: one exhibit carries the slide, the takeaway is one line, detail goes to the appendix or the speaker notes.
