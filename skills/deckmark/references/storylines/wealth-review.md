## Family Office / Wealth Client Review

The periodic review an adviser, wealth manager or family office CIO brings to a family or its investment committee. Where the money is, how it did against the policy, what it cost, whether it is still the right mix, and what needs signing.

**Reader and decision.** Principals and next-generation family members (often not finance professionals), plus the family's investment committee or trustees who are. It drives **stay the course or rebalance**: approve allocation changes, new commitments, liquidity needs, and keep the mandate.

**Length.** 12 to 25 slides or report pages, plus holdings appendix.

**Short form (a quarterly check-in, 5 to 8 pages).** When the material is the custodian statement and the policy: summary page in plain language (total wealth, change, return vs policy benchmark), allocation vs policy ranges, performance table by asset class, liquidity and capital calls due, total cost in money, open items for anything the policy asks to review that the material does not carry.

**Order.**
1. **cover**: family or entity name as the user gives it (or a codename), review period, date, "Private and confidential".
2. **exec-summary-scr**: total wealth now vs last review, return net of fees vs policy benchmark, the one or two things that changed, the decisions requested.
3. **Wealth at a glance**: kpi-strip of total assets, net return period and since inception, liquidity available within 30 days, uncalled private commitments.
4. **Asset allocation vs policy**: stacked-bar or table of actual vs target vs ranges per asset class; breaches highlighted.
5. **Performance**: table by asset class and total: period, YTD, 1/3/5 years and since inception, net of fees, against the stated benchmark for each.
6. **Attribution**: waterfall-bridge of the period's return by allocation and selection, or by asset class contribution.
7. **Private markets**: commitment, called, distributed, NAV, net IRR, TVPI by fund, and the capital call forecast for the next 12 to 24 months.
8. **Liquidity and cash flow**: sources and needs over 12 months (two-column-compare or stacked-bar), including taxes, distributions to family members, and calls.
9. **Risk**: concentration (single stock, sector, manager, currency), drawdown, and look-through exposure; risk-matrix if the user has a register.
10. **Costs**: total cost in currency and basis points: advisory, manager, fund, custody, transaction.
11. **Manager review**: rag-tracker of managers on watch, with reason.
12. **Recommendations and decisions**: recommendation-ask: rebalancing trades, new commitments, policy changes, each with rationale.
13. **Appendix**: holdings, account list, benchmark definitions, glossary for family members.

**What practitioners expect that a model forgets.**
- Net of all fees, with the fee total shown in money, not only basis points.
- Each asset class has its own benchmark, stated; the total portfolio is measured against the policy benchmark in the investment policy statement.
- Private market NAVs lag; the as-of date per fund is shown, and IRRs for young funds carry a "not meaningful" note.
- Liquidity planning includes capital calls and tax dates, not only market value.
- A plain-English glossary for family members, and no unexplained jargon on the summary page.
- Concentrated legacy positions (the family business, a founder stock) are shown, even if held outside the managed mandate.

**Honesty rules.** No return figure without period, gross/net label and benchmark. Holdings data comes from the user's custodian statements; gaps are shown as unpriced. No family member names unless given. No product recommendations that the user did not make.

**How it ends.** The decisions to sign, the next review date, and contacts. Appendix of holdings follows.

**Density.** Sparse to medium on the summary and allocation pages (principals read these), dense in the appendix.

**Words.** 16:9 target 40-60, cap 80 · A4 cap 350 · no prose tables. Body words per content slide (decklint `wordy`, `prose-table`). Principals are often not finance readers: one chart and a line on the summary pages; show it, do not say it: one exhibit carries the slide, the takeaway is one line, detail goes to the appendix or the speaker notes.
