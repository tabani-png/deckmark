## Fund Pitch Deck (GP to LPs)

The fundraising deck. Why this strategy, why this team, the record that proves it, how the fund will be built, and on what terms.

**Reader and decision.** LP investment staff and consultants screening managers; first view forms in the first five slides, then the deck is checked against the DDQ and the PPM. It drives **commit**: take a first meeting, start diligence, and commit an amount to the fund.

**Length.** 15 to 30 slides plus a track-record appendix.

**Short form (a first-meeting deck or thin material, 10 to 14 slides).** When there is no deal-level track record, attribution or case study: cover, disclaimer, fund summary as a col-row of terms, why now with one sourced chart, the edge with its proof number, team as name, role and proof points, the terms the material gives, contacts, open items listing each standard section left out (track record, dispersion, attribution, case studies, portfolio construction, fees) and what would let you build it.

**Order.**
1. **cover**: fund name and number, target size, strategy in one line, date, "Confidential".
2. **disclaimer**: not an offer, PPM governs, past performance, gross/net definitions.
3. **Fund summary**: kpi-strip or term table: target and hard cap, strategy, geography, deal size range, GP commitment, first close date.
4. **Why this strategy now**: market opportunity with a sourced chart, the inefficiency the strategy exploits.
5. **The edge**: sourcing, value creation or underwriting advantage stated as a claim and proved with a number (share of proprietary deals, average entry multiple vs market).
6. **Team**: org-chart with roles, years together, prior deals; key-person list.
7. **Track record summary**: table of prior funds or deals with vintage, size, invested, realised, total value, gross and net MOIC, net IRR, DPI, quartile with source.
8. **Attribution**: who on the current team led each deal; returns earned at prior firms labelled as such.
9. **Dispersion**: ranked-bar or scatter of deal-level MOIC, losses included; loss ratio.
10. **Value creation**: waterfall-bridge of aggregate value creation (revenue growth, margin, multiple, leverage).
11. **Case studies, 2-3 slides**: one winner, one average, ideally one that went wrong and what was learned.
12. **Investment process**: process-chevrons from sourcing to exit, with IC and approval gates.
13. **Portfolio construction**: number of deals, sizing, concentration limits, reserves, pacing.
14. **Risk management and ESG**: only content the user supplies.
15. **Key terms**: management fee, carry, hurdle, catch-up, GP commitment, term, key-person, fee offsets.
16. **Appendix**: deal-by-deal track record, bios, definitions.

**Lean form (default).** Fund summary as a col-row of 4 terms with their figures, not a term table; why now as a ring-pair or a chart with a takeaway-bar; the edge as a big-compare (our entry multiple vs market) or a col-row of proof numbers; team as name, role and 2-3 proof points each, bios to the appendix; value creation as a waterfall-bridge with a takeaway-bar; investment process as a `flow`; a full-bleed statement may open each section. Track-record tables stay dense: they are numbers, which do not count against the budget.

**What practitioners expect that a model forgets.**
- Net returns lead; gross is labelled and never alone.
- Losses are shown. A track record of only winners is not believed.
- Attribution is explicit: which current partner led which deal, and at which firm.
- Realised and unrealised are split; unrealised marks are dated.
- Terms are in the deck. A deck without fees and carry reads as unfavourable terms.
- Quartile claims name the benchmark provider, vintage and as-of date.
- Performance follows the presentation standard the firm claims (GIPS or other) only if the user says it complies.

**Honesty rules.** Every track-record figure comes from the user and carries its as-of date. A missing net figure is shown as a gap, never derived from gross with an assumed fee load. Case studies use the user's facts; company names only if public and given. No "top quartile" without source.

**How it ends.** Key terms, then contacts for investor relations and the fundraising timeline (first close, final close). Appendix follows.

**Density.** Medium. Track-record tables dense and exact; story slides sparse.

**Words.** 16:9 target 40-60, cap 80 · A4 cap 300 · no prose tables. Body words per content slide (decklint `wordy`, `prose-table`). LPs form a view in the first five slides; track-record tables are numbers, which do not count; show it, do not say it: one exhibit carries the slide, the takeaway is one line, detail goes to the appendix or the speaker notes.
