## Board Pack / CFO Board Deck

The finance section of a board meeting. Where we are against plan, what changed, where we are heading, what could go wrong, and what the board must decide today.

**Reader and decision.** Directors with limited context between meetings, including non-executives with finance backgrounds who will read the cash and the forecast closely. It drives **approve**: the specific items on the decision list (budget, capex, facility, dividend, acquisition), and **trust**: confidence that management sees the business clearly.

**Length.** 8 to 15 slides in the body, appendix behind it. Monthly flash: 3 to 5.

**Short form (a monthly flash or thin pack, 6 to 10 slides).** When the material is one month's management accounts and no forecast or risk register: cover, decisions required, kpi-strip over the actual vs budget vs prior-year table, revenue and EBITDA chart-takeaway with the variance named, cash and liquidity headroom, then only the sections the material carries, open items listing each section left out (forecast, risks, covenant compliance) and what would let you build it.

**Order.**
1. **cover**: meeting date, "Board of Directors", agenda item number, paper owner.
2. **Decisions required**: recommendation-ask listing each item, the recommended resolution, and the paper reference. Page 1 or 2, never last.
3. **Finance summary**: exec-summary-scr in board form: on plan or not, the main driver, the outlook change, the ask.
4. **KPI scorecard**: kpi-strip or dashboard-tiles with actual, plan, prior year, latest forecast and RAG for 6 to 12 stable KPIs; the RAG rule in a footnote.
5. **Variance bridge**: waterfall-bridge from budget to actual EBITDA (or revenue), steps named by driver: price, volume, mix, cost, FX, timing.
6. **Income statement**: financial-summary-table, month and YTD, actual vs budget vs prior year, variance columns.
7. **Cash and liquidity**: operating cash flow, cash conversion, working capital days, liquidity headroom (cash plus undrawn facilities); covenant-table if the company has debt.
8. **Where the signal comes from**: small-multiples or ranked-bar by segment, region or unit.
9. **Forecast reset**: full-year forecast vs budget as two-column-compare, assumptions changed.
10. **Scenarios and sensitivity**: base, upside, downside on the two or three drivers that matter.
11. **Strategic initiatives**: rag-tracker of board-approved programmes with spend to date and value delivered vs business case.
12. **Risks**: risk-matrix of material risks with movement since last meeting (arrows).
13. **Decision papers**: one slide per decision: options, recommendation, cost, return, risks.
14. **Appendix**: full P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, capex detail, headcount.

**What practitioners expect that a model forgets.**
- Decisions on page one, with a recommended resolution for each. Boards are not asked open questions.
- Variances explained in the five levels: what happened, why, what we are doing, how far we have got, when it lands and the year-end effect. "Spend was 200k over" is an observation, not an explanation.
- The KPI set is stable. A new metric is introduced with a note; a dropped one is explained.
- Cash before EBITDA. Directors trust cash.
- Favourable and adverse variance colouring follows the sign of the effect on profit, not the sign of the number (cost up is red).
- Prior-period comparatives are restated if definitions changed, with a footnote.

**Honesty rules.** A forecast the user has not given is not extrapolated: the slide shows actuals with `DATA NEEDED: Q3 reforecast`. RAG status needs the threshold. Bad news is stated in the summary, not first appearing on page 9. No "on track" claim without the number against target.

**How it ends.** Decision papers, then appendix. The summary and the decision page agree word for word on what is being asked.

**Density.** Medium. Board members read on tablets and paper: 12px minimum on tables, one exhibit per slide.

**Words.** 16:9 target 50-70, cap 90 · A4 cap 350 · no prose tables. Body words per content slide (decklint `wordy`, `prose-table`). Directors read between meetings: one exhibit, the decision in a line; show it, do not say it: one exhibit carries the slide, the takeaway is one line, detail goes to the appendix or the speaker notes.
