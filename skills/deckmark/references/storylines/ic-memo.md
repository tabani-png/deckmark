## PE Investment Committee Memo

The deal team's case to the IC for buying a company (or for an LP, committing to a fund). Recommendation first, then the thesis the diligence had to prove, then the returns and what could break them.

**Reader and decision.** The investment committee: partners who have not lived the deal and will ask the one question that is not answered. It drives **invest**: approve, approve with conditions, decline, or advance to the next stage.

**Length.** Deck version 15 to 30 slides; memo version 10 to 25 pages plus exhibits. A first-look screening memo is 3 to 5 pages.

**Short form (a first-look screening memo, 3 to 5 pages or 6 to 10 slides).** When the material is a teaser or a CIM and no diligence has run: recommendation (advance, pass, or advance with questions) with the default outcome, the business in one line and its numbers, the thesis pillars as questions diligence must answer, entry price and a returns sense-check only if the material gives price and a case, key risks tagged, the questions for the next stage. No diligence tracker, no value creation plan.

**Order.**
1. **cover**: project codename, IC stage (first look, final), date, deal team.
2. **recommendation-ask**: the vote, equity cheque, entry EV and multiple, base IRR and MOIC, the two or three reasons, conditions.
3. **exec-summary-scr**: company in one line, thesis pillars, key risks, why now.
4. **Investment thesis**: 3 to 4 pillars, each a claim diligence had to prove, each with the evidence status.
5. **Company overview**: kpi-strip and revenue mix stacked-bar.
6. **Market and position**: market growth, share, competitive matrix-2x2.
7. **Transaction overview**: sources-uses (debt at net proceeds after OID and fees; how any gap is funded), cap-table (pro forma leverage), ownership and governance terms.
8. **Financials**: financial-summary-table, historicals and the base case; waterfall-bridge of reported to QoE-adjusted EBITDA.
9. **Cases**: base, upside, downside in a two-column-compare or table with the assumptions that differ.
10. **Returns**: waterfall-bridge of value creation entry to exit (EBITDA growth, multiple, deleveraging); sensitivity-table of IRR vs entry and exit multiple.
11. **Diligence findings**: rag-tracker by workstream (commercial, financial, legal, tax, IT, ESG) with the finding and its effect on the case.
12. **Risks**: risk-matrix with every flag tagged CHANGES THE DECISION / WORTH A QUESTION / EXPLAINED BY CONTEXT.
13. **Value creation plan**: 100-day plan as timeline-gantt, levers tied to the bridge.
14. **Exit**: routes, buyer universe, timing, exit multiple vs entry.
15. **Appendix**: model outputs, management assessment, data room index, open items.

**What practitioners expect that a model forgets.**
- The thesis comes before the checks. A check that does not bear on the thesis cannot change the verdict.
- The value creation bridge splits returns into EBITDA growth, multiple expansion and deleveraging. ICs distrust returns that come mostly from multiple expansion.
- The downside case is real (named shock, stated EBITDA decline) and shows covenant headroom and equity loss.
- Diligence basis is stated: what was reviewed, what was not, open items at the time of the memo.
- Dissent and what would reverse the recommendation are recorded.
- Rollover equity and management incentive plan are shown in the ownership table.

**Honesty rules.** Neutral stance: never "assume the seller flatters", never "silence is a finding". A missing document is a WORTH A QUESTION item, not a verdict. Hard blocks only for numbers that do not tie or integrity failures; a block stops the verdict, not the analysis, and the page states what happens by default if the IC does nothing. Before shipping, the memo layout is tested on a known pass case and a known decline case.

**How it ends.** The vote requested again, conditions to close, open items with owners and dates, then the appendix.

**Density.** Dense. Memo format favours memo-page with inset exhibits.

**Words.** 16:9 target 100-130, cap 150 · A4 cap 350. Body words per content slide (decklint `wordy`). A judgment memo: the reasoning is the product, but each page still proves one point; show it, do not say it: one exhibit carries the slide, the takeaway is one line, detail goes to the appendix or the speaker notes.
