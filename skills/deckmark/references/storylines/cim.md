## Sell-Side CIM

The confidential information memorandum. The full, named story of a business for sale, sent after an NDA, written so a buyer can submit an indicative offer without a management meeting first.

**Reader and decision.** Corporate development teams and PE deal teams (associates read every page; partners read the summary, the highlights and the financials). The CIM drives **bid**: a non-binding indication of interest by a stated date.

**Length.** 30 to 60 pages, landscape deck or portrait report. A lower-mid-market CIM can be 25.

**Short form (CIM summary or thin material, 10 to 16 slides).** When the brief asks for a summary, or the material has the financials but not the market, customer, management or operations data: cover, disclaimer, exec summary (KPI tiles over the claims, each with its number), investment highlights as `.summary-rows` with the proof number and page (never "Claim 1 ... 6" cards), historical financials table, revenue and EBITDA chart-takeaway, adjusted EBITDA bridge, then only the sections the material carries, process and next steps, open items listing each section left out and what would let you build it. Fewer honest slides beat a full-length spine of half-empty pages.

**Order.**
1. **cover**: "Project <codename>", "Confidential Information Memorandum", advisor role, date.
2. **disclaimer**: confidentiality, no representation or warranty, forward-looking statements, contact the advisor only.
3. **exec-summary-scr**: what the company does, LTM revenue and adjusted EBITDA with growth, the reason for sale, the transaction sought (100% / majority), the bid date.
4. **Investment highlights, 1-2 pages**: 5 to 7 claims, each with its proof number and a page reference to where it is proved.
5. **Company overview**: history timeline-gantt, ownership org-chart, locations map, headcount.
6. **Products and services**: stacked-bar of revenue by product line over 3-5 years; gross margin by line.
7. **Customers**: concentration ranked-bar (top 10 as Customer A, B, C), retention or cohort chart, contract length, recurring share.
8. **Market**: market size and growth (one credible third-party source), marimekko or ranked-bar of competitive position, 2-3 drivers.
9. **Operations**: sites, capacity and utilisation, suppliers, systems.
10. **Management and organisation**: org-chart by role and tenure; who stays post-close.
11. **Historical financials**: financial-summary-table (3-5 years plus LTM), revenue and EBITDA chart-takeaway.
12. **Adjusted EBITDA bridge**: waterfall-bridge from reported to adjusted, every add-back named and sized.
13. **Balance sheet and cash flow**: NWC as % of revenue by quarter, capex split maintenance vs growth, cash conversion.
14. **Projections**: financial-summary-table with A/E split, the assumptions table, budget vs actual for the last year.
15. **Growth opportunities**: each sized, with its cost and timing.
16. **Process and next steps**: process-chevrons, timetable, IOI content requested, advisor contacts.
17. **Appendix**: full statements, reconciliations, definitions.

**Lean form (default for slide CIMs).** Visual first, the way bank-made decks do it: investment highlights as a numbered list of 5-7 one-line claims with one proof number each (or a col-row), never label | paragraph rows; company overview as a col-row of proof numbers and a timeline; customers and market as charts with 3-5 short bold-led bullets beside them; the business model as a `flow`; management as name, role and 2-3 proof points, full bios to the appendix; history as a timeline, not prose. Financial pages stay tables of numbers. The cap is 160 body words: that is room for a chart and short bullets, not for paragraphs.

**What practitioners expect that a model forgets.**
- Customer names are withheld: Customer A, B, C. Real names wait for a later diligence stage.
- Every highlight traces to a number a data room document will hold. The QoE will reconcile every figure later.
- Adjusted and reported EBITDA are both shown; each add-back is itemised, never one "normalisations" line.
- Budget vs actual for the latest year: buyers look for a management team that hits its plan.
- Growth opportunities carry their cost; free-lunch upside is discounted to zero.
- Maintenance vs growth capex is split; buyers model cash, not EBITDA.
- Fiscal year end, currency and LTM date are stated on every financial page.
- Where a transaction structure or financing is shown, sources are cash: debt at net proceeds after OID and fees, never face value, with any gap's funding stated.

**Honesty rules.** No invented market size; use the user's source or a marked gap. Projections are labelled "Management projections" with the date prepared. A highlight with no supporting number is cut, not softened. Any page that would name a customer, employee or supplier the user did not clear uses roles or letters.

**How it ends.** Process instructions: bid date, what the IOI must contain (value, structure, financing, diligence needs, approvals), advisor contacts. Then the appendix.

**Density.** Medium. Report pages carry 2 exhibits and running text; deck pages carry one exhibit and a sidebar.

**Words.** 16:9 target 80-140, cap 160 · A4 cap 400 · no prose tables. Body words per content slide (decklint `wordy`, `prose-table`). Real sell-side CIMs run 150-230 words a page; they carry it as a chart with direct labels plus 3-5 short bold-led bullets, a KPI band over a chart, a numbered highlight list with one proof number each, or tables of numbers, never as label | paragraph grids; show it, do not say it: one exhibit carries the slide, the takeaway is one line, detail goes to the appendix or the speaker notes.
