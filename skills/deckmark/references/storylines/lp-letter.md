## LP Quarterly Letter

The GP's quarterly report to limited partners: a letter from the managing partners, the fund's performance, what happened in the portfolio, and the capital account. Same layout every quarter.

**Reader and decision.** LP investment staff (pension, endowment, fund of funds, family office) who monitor dozens of funds and compare quarter to quarter. It drives **hold and re-up**: keep confidence in the manager, fund the next call, and commit to the next fund.

**Length.** 8 to 25 pages, portrait report or landscape deck. A small fund letter can be 4 pages plus statements.

**Short form (a short quarterly update, 4 to 6 pages).** When the quarter's material is the fund's performance table and a few portfolio notes: the letter's opening (what happened and what it means), the performance table with as-of date and net figures, portfolio activity as a short table (new, follow-on, exits), the outlook only if the managers wrote one, open items for anything a standard letter carries that the material does not (valuation methodology changes, capital calls).

**Order.**
1. **cover**: fund name, quarter and date, "Confidential, for limited partners only".
2. **Letter from the GP, 1-2 pages** (memo-page): what happened this quarter, the market in two paragraphs, activity (new investments, exits, calls, distributions), what is next. Signed by roles named by the user.
3. **Fund at a glance**: kpi-strip of committed capital, called %, distributed, NAV, net IRR, TVPI, DPI, RVPI, each with the prior quarter in small type.
4. **Performance**: net IRR, TVPI and DPI by quarter since inception (line or bar), gross-to-net bridge if the user gives fees and carry; benchmark (PME or quartile) only with its source.
5. **Cash flows**: contributions and distributions by quarter (stacked-bar, negatives below the axis), uncalled commitment.
6. **Portfolio summary**: appendix-table style in the body: company, date, invested, realised, unrealised, total value, gross MOIC, gross IRR, status. Realised and unrealised subtotals.
7. **Valuation bridge**: waterfall-bridge of NAV from last quarter (calls, distributions, value change split into operating and multiple where given, FX).
8. **Portfolio company pages, 1 each for material holdings**: kpi-strip (revenue, EBITDA, net leverage, LTM growth), one paragraph on the quarter, valuation method and multiple.
9. **Pipeline and exits**: process status as rag-tracker, no names unless announced.
10. **ESG and team**: only if the user supplies content.
11. **Capital account statement**: the LP's own beginning balance, contributions, distributions, fees, expenses, carry allocation, gain/loss, ending balance.
12. **Fees and expenses**: management fee, offsets, partnership expenses, carry; follows the industry fee reporting template format where the user's fund uses it.
13. **disclaimer**: unaudited figures, valuation policy reference, past performance.

**What practitioners expect that a model forgets.**
- Same pages, same order, same definitions every quarter. A changed KPI set or a moved metric reads as hiding something.
- Gross and net are always labelled. Net figures lead.
- DPI matters more as funds age; a late-life fund that leads with IRR and buries DPI is read as evasive.
- Valuation method and the movement in the multiple are disclosed per holding; write-downs are named, not netted.
- Subscription line use is disclosed; it inflates early IRR.
- Figures are "unaudited" except at year end.

**Honesty rules.** No performance figure without its as-of date and gross/net label. No quartile or benchmark claim without the source and vintage. Holdings with no update this quarter say "No update provided" rather than a filler paragraph. Exits and new deals stay unnamed unless publicly announced and given by the user.

**How it ends.** Capital account statement, fees and expenses, then the disclaimer. The letter itself closes with the next reporting date and a contact for investor relations.

**Density.** Medium. Tables dominate; prose is confined to the letter and one paragraph per company.

**Words.** 16:9 target 80-110, cap 130 · A4 cap 350. Body words per content slide (decklint `wordy`). The letter page is prose by design; performance pages are tables; show it, do not say it: one exhibit carries the slide, the takeaway is one line, detail goes to the appendix or the speaker notes.
