## SCR Strategy Deck

The consulting answer deck. One governing thought at the top, three to five claims under it, evidence under each. The titles alone tell the whole argument.

**Reader and decision.** A CEO, CFO or steering committee choosing a direction: enter, exit, fix, invest, restructure. The deck drives **adopt**: approve the recommendation and fund the next phase.

**Length.** 12 to 25 slides in the body; appendix as long as the evidence needs. A steering-committee update can be 8.

**Short form (an answer-first summary, 5 to 8 slides).** When the material supports the governing thought and fewer than three claims: cover, exec summary (situation, complication, resolution with the number), one slide per supported claim with its exhibit, the recommendation and next steps, open items listing each claim the material cannot yet prove and what would.

**Order.**
1. **cover**: the question as a title or a codename, date, draft/final.
2. **exec-summary-scr**: Situation (the undisputed fact, with its number), Complication (the number that moved or the decision that is due), Resolution (the recommendation and its value). Carries the size of the prize.
3. **agenda-tracker**: the key line as 3 to 5 section names written as claims.
4. **Situation, 1-3 slides**: chart-takeaway or stacked-bar showing the baseline (share, margin, growth vs peers). Must carry the metric the complication will move.
5. **Complication, 2-4 slides**: ranked-bar, waterfall-bridge or small-multiples showing what changed and how big the gap is. One slide must quantify the cost of doing nothing.
6. **issue-tree** (optional): how the problem was broken down; leaves sized.
7. **Options, 1-2 slides**: harvey-table or matrix-2x2 scoring 3 to 4 options on stated criteria, including "do nothing".
8. **Recommendation and value, 2-4 slides**: waterfall-bridge from today to target (EBITDA, cost, revenue), each lever sized with its range.
9. **Risks and what must be true**: risk-matrix or two-column-compare (what we believe / how we would know we are wrong).
10. **Roadmap**: timeline-gantt or process-chevrons, 30/60/90 or wave plan, owners.
11. **recommendation-ask**: decisions requested today and next steps with dates.
12. **Appendix**: methodology, data definitions, interview list (roles only), detailed tables.

**What practitioners expect that a model forgets.**
- The governing thought can be contradicted. "Optimise the portfolio" is a topic; "Exit two of five segments to lift group margin 3 points" is a thought.
- Each section opens with a slide whose title is the section's claim, not its name.
- The key line is MECE: no two claims overlap, and the residual is named.
- A "do nothing" baseline sits beside every option, with its cost.
- A ghost-deck status on each slide while drafting: confirmed, in progress, or missing, in the STORYBOARD.md line format of SKILL.md Step 2. The status shows in the storyboard and the handoff, never on the slide.
- Interview and survey evidence states n and the date.
- Ranges, not points, for sized levers. Sum of levers ties to the bridge total.

**Honesty rules.** Sizing that the user did not give is not estimated silently: the lever shows as a hatched bar with `DATA NEEDED: baseline cost by site`. Benchmarks without a named source are cut. Never cite "industry research shows" with no source line. Client and competitor names appear only if given.

**How it ends.** The decisions asked for today, one line each with an owner and a date, then the appendix divider. Not "Thank you", not "Questions?".

**Density.** Medium to dense. Two lines of title, one exhibit, one sidebar. A slide that needs three exhibits is two slides.

**Words.** 16:9 target 60-80, cap 100 · A4 cap 350 · no prose tables. Body words per content slide (decklint `wordy`, `prose-table`). One exhibit and a short sidebar; the argument lives in the titles; show it, do not say it: one exhibit carries the slide, the takeaway is one line, detail goes to the appendix or the speaker notes.
