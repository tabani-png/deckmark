## Teaser

The blind one-to-two pager that goes to a wide buyer list before any NDA. It describes the business without naming it and asks one thing: sign the NDA.

**Reader and decision.** A screener at a PE fund or a corporate development analyst, reading dozens a week, deciding in about two minutes whether the deal fits the mandate (size, sector, geography, margin). The teaser drives **engage**: request the NDA and the CIM.

**Length.** 1 to 2 pages, portrait report format, sometimes 3 to 4 landscape slides.

**Order (two-page version).**
1. **Masthead band**: "Project <codename>", one-line descriptor ("A leading provider of X to Y in Z region"), advisor, date, "Strictly private and confidential".
2. **Business overview (page 1, left)**: 4 to 6 sentences: what it sells, to whom, how it earns, where. No names, no towns that identify it.
3. **kpi-strip (page 1, top or right)**: revenue, EBITDA, EBITDA margin, growth (CAGR over a stated period), recurring %, employees. Each with the period and currency.
4. **Investment highlights (page 1)**: 4 to 6 short claims, each with a number.
5. **Financial summary (page 2)**: small financial-summary-table, 3 historical years plus current-year budget or LTM, revenue, gross profit, EBITDA, margin.
6. **Chart (page 2)**: one stacked-bar of revenue mix or a revenue and EBITDA bar chart.
7. **Transaction overview (page 2)**: what is offered (100% / majority / growth capital), seller intent (full exit, rollover), management's plans.
8. **Next steps and contacts**: NDA request, advisor names and emails as given.

**Lean form (default).** Highlights are one line and one number each; the business overview is 4-6 short sentences, not a paragraph block; the landscape version leads with a col-row or KPI strip and one chart with a takeaway-bar.

**What practitioners expect that a model forgets.**
- Blind means blind: no company name, no founder name, no exact city, no unique product name, no customer named, no award that identifies the firm. Ranges or rounded figures are normal ("revenue of c. USD 40m").
- Screeners look first at size (revenue, EBITDA), margin, growth, and sector fit. Those numbers sit above the fold of page 1.
- "Adjusted EBITDA" carries a footnote saying what is adjusted.
- No valuation or price expectation.
- The descriptor line must be specific enough to screen ("B2B route-based testing services, US Southeast"), vague enough not to identify.

**Honesty rules.** Rounded is fine; invented is not. If the user gave no EBITDA, the tile says `DATA NEEDED` and the page is not shipped. Highlights such as "market leader" need a basis (share, rank, source) or are rewritten as a fact ("No. 2 by revenue in its regional market, per <source>"). A teaser that could identify the company is a confidentiality failure; decklint flags proper nouns that match the user's real company name.

**How it ends.** Contacts and the NDA call to action. Nothing after it.

**Density.** Dense but scannable: a KPI strip, two short text blocks, one table, one chart.

**Words.** 16:9 target 40-60, cap 80 · A4 cap 350 · no prose tables. Body words per content slide (decklint `wordy`, `prose-table`). A screener reads it in two minutes (real two-page teasers run 240-340 words a page): numbers above the fold, highlights one line each; show it, do not say it: one exhibit carries the slide, the takeaway is one line, detail goes to the appendix or the speaker notes.
