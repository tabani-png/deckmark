## Credit Memo (Private Credit / Direct Lending)

The lender's case for a loan. How much, on what terms, how the borrower repays, what protects the lender if it does not, and how far the business can fall before that protection is tested.

**Reader and decision.** The credit committee: senior credit officers who read the first page and the downside case, then the covenant definitions. It drives **lend**: approve, approve with conditions, restructure, or decline, at a stated hold size.

**Length.** Screening memo 3 to 6 pages; final credit memo 15 to 35 pages or 15 to 25 slides.

**Short form (thin material, 8 to 13 slides).** When the material carries the terms and the financials but not the industry, sponsor, collateral or a downside model: cover, recommendation-ask (with the default-outcome line), deal snapshot (KPI tiles above the financial summary they come from), financial analysis, sources and uses, covenant table with headroom on what the material supports, risks tagged, open items (every storyline section not built, with the document that would let you build it), close. Do not build half-empty pages for the missing sections; they are rows on the open-items page. The recommendation states which missing sections CHANGE THE DECISION.

**Order.**
1. **cover**: borrower codename, facility, amount, sponsor, committee date.
2. **recommendation-ask**: facility type and amount, hold size, pricing (margin, floor, OID, fees), tenor, amortisation, security, the recommendation label, the two or three reasons, the risk that is the deal.
3. **Deal snapshot**: kpi-strip of LTM EBITDA, total and senior net leverage, interest cover, fixed charge cover, LTV (EV-based), sponsor equity %.
4. **Business overview**: revenue mix, recurring share, customer concentration ranked-bar.
5. **Industry**: cyclicality, competitors, barriers; how the business did in the last downturn.
6. **Sponsor**: track record with similar credits, equity cheque, support history.
7. **Financial analysis**: financial-summary-table with EBITDA, FCF, capex, working capital, leverage and coverage by year; waterfall-bridge of reported to lender-adjusted EBITDA with each add-back judged.
8. **Sources and uses / capitalisation**: sources-uses (net proceeds after OID and fees, never face) and cap-table with cumulative leverage through each tranche.
9. **Repayment and debt capacity**: FCF to debt service, deleveraging path, refinance test at maturity.
10. **Downside case**: named shock, EBITDA decline, leverage and coverage, liquidity runway; EBITDA cushion to covenant breach and to default.
11. **covenant-table**: each covenant, definition reference, level with step-downs, headroom in base and downside, first test date.
12. **Collateral and structure**: security package, ranking, guarantors, org-chart of the legal structure showing where debt sits.
13. **Risks and mitigants**: risk-matrix, each tagged CHANGES THE DECISION / WORTH A QUESTION / EXPLAINED BY CONTEXT.
14. **Appendix**: spreads, comps, KYC/ESG screening, documents reviewed.

**What practitioners expect that a model forgets.**
- Adjusted EBITDA definitions decide whether covenants bind. Uncapped add-backs are called out by name.
- Headroom is shown against the downside, not only the base.
- A covenant failed on actual figures is labelled day-one default, never headroom.
- Leverage is shown gross and net, total and senior, and it states which EBITDA (reported, adjusted, pro forma).
- "Looks wrong, is normal": amortising balances fall; a new loan carries the underwriting appraisal; asset-backed loans can lack cash flow cover by design; PIK toggles are a feature to price, not automatically a red flag.
- Covenant-lite deals say so on page 1.
- **Sources are cash, never face value.** A loan issued at an OID raises face times the issue price: $85.0M at 98.0 is 83.3 of proceeds, 1.7 short of 85.0 of uses. Sources show net proceeds (or face plus a separate negative OID line), fees paid at close are uses, and the slide states how the gap is funded (sponsor equity, balance-sheet cash, a larger facility). A sources-and-uses that ties at face with an OID on the term sheet is wrong even when decklint's `sources-uses` check passes.
- The default outcome if the committee does nothing is one line on the recommendation slide and again on the close.

**Honesty rules.** Neutral: no "look for reasons to reject". Missing financial year = hard stop for that section, with the document request list shown. Hard blocks only where numbers do not tie or integrity fails; curable gaps become conditions. The recommendation label is a draft for the committee.

**How it ends.** The recommendation label and conditions precedent again, open items with owners, monitoring plan (reporting frequency, triggers). Then appendix.

**Density.** Dense. Tables at the theme's minimum size; one exhibit per page in memo format.

**Words.** 16:9 target 100-130, cap 150 · A4 cap 350. Body words per content slide (decklint `wordy`). A judgment memo: the reasoning is the product, but each page still proves one point; show it, do not say it: one exhibit carries the slide, the takeaway is one line, detail goes to the appendix or the speaker notes.
